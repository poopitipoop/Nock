01 — The Protocol

Bitcoin turned electricity into money.
Pearl turns compute into money.

Bitcoin's proof-of-work showed the world that scarce, verifiable energy could become digital scarcity. But its hashing is artificial — work invented purely to be hard. Pearl replaces that wasted hashing with the one operation every GPU on Earth already exists to perform: matrix multiplication. Mining Pearl and running AI training or inference become, as much as possible, the same act.

◈

AI-Native Mining

Block rewards are minted in direct proportion to verifiable MatMul operations — the exact computation underlying every neural network on the planet.

◇

Dual-Utility Yield

The same GPU cycle that trains or serves a model can simultaneously mine a block. Security and useful work are no longer competing for energy — they share it.

◆

Economically ASIC-Resistant

A chip built only to mine Pearl loses the ability to do useful AI work, and is therefore strictly less profitable than a general-purpose accelerator.

◉

Zero-Knowledge Privacy

Model weights and proprietary data never leave the miner. Proofs attest that the work happened — without revealing what was computed.

02 — The Pearlification Process

How a grain of compute becomes a Pearl

Every real pearl forms in four stages: irritant, nacre, patient grinding, and lustre. The Pearl protocol mirrors this exactly — it's not just a metaphor, it's the literal mechanism (Section 3 of the whitepaper).

  1. 01

    The Grain

    An oyster needs an irritant to begin. Pearl needs a workload: two real matrices, A and B, drawn from an actual AI training or inference job. This is the raw, unglamorous grain of sand the whole process is built around.

  2. 02

    The Nacre

    The oyster secretes layer upon layer of nacre around the grain. Pearl deterministically generates low-rank noise matrices E and F, seeded from a cryptographic commitment to A, B, and the chain state, and adds them to the workload. The result is uniformly hard to compute — indistinguishable from pure randomness — so no miner can cheat by picking easy inputs.

  3. 03

    The Tumble

    Layer formation takes time and repetition. Miners grind the noised matrices tile by tile, accumulating a running hash of each block's intermediate values, searching for the one tile whose BLAKE3 digest slips beneath the difficulty target — a winning lottery ticket earned only through real, verifiable computation.

  4. 04

    The Lustre

    A jeweler cracks nothing open — the pearl is simply drawn out, whole. Pearl peels the noise back through a quick, exact correction, recovering the true product A·B, while a zk-SNARK (Plonky2, hash-based, post-quantum) proves the entire process was done honestly — without ever revealing A or B themselves. What's left is lustrous: a valid block, a minted reward, and a completed unit of real AI work.

03 — The Loop

A virtuous loop, not a zero-sum race

Classical proof-of-work monetizes security alone. Pearl monetizes security and utility, so the payout for defending the network can subsidize genuinely useful compute.

1

Market demand for AI increases the value of the mining subsidy.

2

Higher reward value incentivizes deploying more useful compute onto the network.

3

Greater compute power hardens the network's consensus and monetary credibility.

4

Mining produces real inference and training output that accrues real economic value.

Emission decay — no halving cliffs

No halving shocks

Bitcoin's emission halves overnight every four years, creating incentive cliffs and volatility. Pearl instead decays smoothly, polynomially, block by block — the remaining supply falls like 1/t rather than dropping in steps. Early miners are rewarded, but the tail stays thick enough to keep funding long-run security.

WTEMA difficulty

Rather than Bitcoin's 2016-block retarget window, Pearl re-tunes difficulty every block with a Weighted-Target Exponential Moving Average, keeping block time stable without the whiplash of infrequent, large corrections.

05 — Under the Nacre

Technical specifications

Hash-based zk-SNARKs

Built on Plonky2 — no trusted setup, transparent, and secure against quantum adversaries since it relies only on hash functions, not elliptic-curve assumptions. Three-layer recursion compresses proofs to under 60KB.

Post-quantum addresses

Standardized on Taproot, with an XMSS hash-based signature path (OP_CHECKXMSSSIG) and Pay-to-Merkle-Root outputs reserved for activation if Schnorr signatures are ever weakened by quantum computers.

Low-rank noise paradigm

Noise matrices are constructed with rank r, so the correction terms cost only O(n²r) operations — letting the true product be recovered almost for free once the noised multiplication is done.

Tile-level commitments

Matrices are committed as BLAKE3 Merkle trees, row-major for A, column-major for B — keeping opening proofs small and letting B (often known in advance, as with model weights) be pre-noised once per chain-state update.

Dynamic block certificates

The 80-byte Bitcoin header's fixed nonce is replaced with a variable-length, version-tagged block certificate, capped at 65KB, decoupling block identity from the randomized zk-witness itself.

OP_CAT re-enabled

A resource-bounded concatenation opcode is restored to the script VM, widening the space of programmable spending conditions available to Pearl contracts.

06 — What's Next

From exact integers to the whole AI stack

Today's Pearl kernel is an exact INT MatMul — a drop-in for integer inference. Most modern training and inference, however, runs in low-precision floating point (BF16, FP8, FP4). A future PoUW upgrade is already in development to bring that world on-chain.

Live

Exact INT MatMul mining

Drop-in proof-of-useful-work for integer inference workloads, in production today.

In research

Quantization-native PoUW

A high-accuracy approximate MatMul that doubles as a plug-in quantization library, using native quantization noise itself as the PoW perturbation.

Planned

Training workload onboarding

Extending mining eligibility to BF16 training, not just inference.

Vision

A marketplace of useful compute

Native on-chain settlement of compute contracts — unifying tokens mined across different hardware and precisions into one, and tying Pearl's value directly to demand for real-world AI compute.
